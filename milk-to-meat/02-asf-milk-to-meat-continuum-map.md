# **ohASF Milk-to-Meat Continuum Map**

Nov 9, 2026 · Steven Thorsen

## **BLUF**

Our Adult Spiritual Formation (ASF) approach covers the continuum's milk column well for the gospel, faith, Scripture, fellowship and prayer. It doesn't teach the Hebrews 6 ordinances (washings/baptism, laying on of hands) or the Lord's Supper, though we baptize and take communion regularly, and it's thin on judgment. Its meat column is mostly empty. That's by design: Foundations, our six-course spine, is built for newcomers. Our deeper material (Sproul, Keller, the catechumenate) is only in planning research. None of it's written as a class yet, so Pathway B has nothing to run.

The two frameworks fit together. ASF is organized by practice (seven practices over two years); the continuum is organized by truth. Hebrews 5:14 gives solid food to those trained διὰ τὴν ἕξιν (through the habit/practice), so our practice ecology is how milk becomes meat, and the continuum names what each practice has to deepen into.

Recommendation: start Pathway A with the gospel as its Starting Point, keep Foundations as Pathway A's spine, add two short milk modules to fill the ordinance and last-things gaps, move baptism from Year Two to the front, build nine meat modules for Pathway B, and place long-time members topic by topic through a sponsor-led conversation, not a test.

## **Gaps, overlaps, and what ASF adds**

The biggest gap is the ordinances: three of the eleven topics, and ASF carries them only as a guardrail sentence ("keep… baptism, communion… central"). No module teaches them.

### **Gaps, in priority order**

1. **The ordinances aren't taught.** Hebrews 6:2 calls them βαπτισμῶν διδαχῆς (of-washings teaching), so the foundation is instruction about the ordinance, not just the act. The same goes for ἐπιθέσεως τε χειρῶν (and of-laying-on of-hands) and for the breaking of bread in Acts 2:42.  
2. **Baptism sits in Year Two.** The invite lists it under "Personal Testimony / Witness (incl. baptism)." Acts puts it at the front (2:41; 8:36–38; 16:33), and our June catechumenate study landed on "come now, be baptized, then be formed." Move it to Pathway A's first stage.  
3. **Last things are thin.** Judgment barely appears, and "forever home" leans toward heaven as destination. Paul's hope is a raised body: ἀπαρχὴ τῶν κεκοιμημένων (firstfruits of-the ones-having-fallen-asleep), 1 Cor 15:20, "But now Christ is risen from the dead, and has become the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep." The Nicene Creed confesses the resurrection of the dead too.  
4. **The meat column is almost empty.** Faith under suffering, the Melchizedek priesthood, and spiritual warfare appear nowhere, not even in the research.  
5. **No doctrinal catechesis.** The catechumenate had candidates memorize the creed. We use the Nicene Creed and WCF only to vet resources. ἡ διδαχὴ τῶν ἀποστόλων (the teaching of-the apostles), Acts 2:42, is content as well as a reading skill. Faircreek's elders once planned a "Christian Beliefs" series for this; it was never staffed.  
6. **No module trains people to disciple others,** though our teaching teams already do it. That's the 2 Tim 2:2 hinge for Pathway B. North Point's LDG already models the shape: mentor one-on-ones, a "Pay it forward" month, and graduates nominating the next cohort. It's men-only and marriage-heavy, so it'd need adapting.

An earlier Mark 9 exegesis bears on \#4 and on the fasting review. NKJV's "prayer and fasting" (9:29) follows the Textus Receptus; the critical text has prayer alone. Settle that before fasting is taught off that verse.

### **Overlaps**

* **C4W2 "Our New Reality" is the densest session.** It feeds union with Christ (\#3), assurance (\#6) and resurrection hope (\#5), so it's the natural place to deepen first.  
* **C3W2 "The Son" carries both the gospel (\#0) and the resurrection (\#5).** That's fine at milk level.  
* **Bible reading is taught twice (C5W2, then C6).** The brief plans it that way.  
* **Inside the continuum, \#0 and \#6 meet at meat level.** Justification and atonement are adjacent doctrines, so teach them together. \#1 and \#2 are paired in Mark 1:15 and Heb 6:1 and should stay paired at milk.

## **Two pathways on one continuum**

Both pathways run on the same eleven topics and end at the same place: commissioning to multiply. They differ only in where a person starts. Heb 5:12, ὀφείλοντες εἶναι διδάσκαλοι διὰ τὸν χρόνον (owing to-be teachers because-of the time), is the reason Pathway B exists: years in church don't place anyone.

### **Pathway A: new believer, foundation first (8 three-week modules, then Year Two)**

1. **Welcome** (house Foyer), with Starting Point: the gospel first (topic 0), before any milk. Assign a sponsor at the first step; the catechumenate sponsor is our Apprenticeship practice with a name on it.  
2. **Course 1, How to Follow Jesus:** repentance and faith (\#1, \#2) under the kingdom.  
3. **New milk module, "Baptism and the Table":** what washings and baptism mean, what the Supper means, belonging and blessing (\#3, \#9, \#4). It closes with baptism for anyone not yet baptized. This is where baptism moves out of Year Two.  
4. **Courses 2–4** (Story, Who God Is, New Identity): resurrection (\#5) and fellowship (\#8), with the gospel deepened from Starting Point.  
5. **New milk module, "Hope and Account":** the raised body, everyone giving account, and where assurance rests (\#5, \#6).  
6. **Courses 5–6** (Habits, Read the Bible Well): prayer (\#10) and the apostles' teaching (\#7).  
7. **Year Two "Sent" with the sponsor:** serving, witness, generosity. At the end, the same conversation used in Pathway B places them for meat modules. That's our version of mystagogy (the catechumenate's after-baptism teaching).

### **Pathway B: long-time member, assessed first**

1. **Entry conversation** with a sponsor or elder couple, placing the person on each topic (next section).  
2. **Milk only where needed.** The person takes the single Foundations week or new milk module that covers a weak topic, not the whole spine.  
3. **Meat modules** (below), chosen from the placement.  
4. **Practice paired with each module.** Meat comes διὰ τὴν ἕξιν (through the practice), with faculties γεγυμνασμένα (having-been-trained), Heb 5:14. So intercession means leading a prayer group, and cutting straight means leading a passage study. The vehicle is LDP, Faircreek's Leadership Development Program, which the Discipleship Flywheel calls Leadership Development Groups: groups of four with a facilitator and a mentor, and a month serving in a ministry each phase. One meat module braids into each LDP module, as laid out in the LDP Milk-to-Meat Transformation Map.  
5. **Commissioning** with laying on of hands (Acts 13:3; 1 Tim 4:14), at LDP's commencement at Align, the twice-yearly leaders' gathering, into teaching teams, sponsorship or group leadership: "And the things that you have heard from me among many witnesses, commit these to faithful men who will be able to teach others also" (2 Tim 2:2). Pathway A's sponsors come from here, which closes the loop.

### **M9 anchor passage: Acts 3:1–10**

Peter and John hand a man lame from birth what they've been given, not what they own. That's M9 in one scene: a gift received, then given away in Jesus's name, by two disciples serving side by side.

***1** Now Peter and John went up together to the temple at the hour of prayer, the ninth hour. **2** And a certain man lame from his mother’s womb was carried, whom they laid daily at the gate of the temple which is called Beautiful, to ask alms from those who entered the temple; **3** who, seeing Peter and John about to go into the temple, asked for alms. **4** And fixing his eyes on him, with John, Peter said, “Look at us.” **5** So he gave them his attention, expecting to receive something from them. **6** Then Peter said, “Silver and gold I do not have, but what I do have I give you: In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, rise up and walk.” **7** And he took him by the right hand and lifted him up, and immediately his feet and ankle bones received strength. **8** So he, leaping up, stood and walked and entered the temple with them—walking, leaping, and praising God. **9** And all the people saw him walking and praising God. **10** Then they knew that it was he who sat begging alms at the Beautiful Gate of the temple; and they were filled with wonder and amazement at what had happened to him. (Acts 3:1–10)*

**Discussion questions**

1. Peter had no silver or gold, but he gave what he'd been given. What has God put in your hands (a gift, a hard-won experience, your standing in Christ) that you hold back because it doesn't feel like enough?  
2. The man asked for money and walked away on his own feet. Where are people around you asking for less than Christ offers, and how do we meet the real need without brushing off the one they named?  
3. Peter spoke “in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth,” then took the man by the hand. In your serving and leading, how do you tell ministry done in Jesus's name from ministry done on your own strength or reputation?

## **God's house at Faircreek and our flow**

Faircreek's "For the One in 2026" house is the *where*; our flow is *what* gets formed there. Its ring runs Neighborhood → build trust → Foyer → be a guide → Living Room → cultivate community → Kitchen → develop depth → back to Neighborhood, with Next Generation (Kids Street, Student Ministry, Young Adults) at the center. Next Generation becomes a band wrapped right around Christ, inside the outer ring: every age gathered close to him and moving through every room. Our version is titled "God's house at Faircreek: For the One in 2027."

## **The Five Faith Catalysts on our map**

Faircreek already teaches Andy Stanley's Five Faith Catalysts in LDP Phase One, Module 1: the five things God uses to grow faith. They line up with our map almost one-to-one, so they're a ready bridge for leaders who already know them. The catalysts say *how* faith grows; the map says *what* it grows into, the milk of Hebrews 6:1–2 and Acts 2:42 taken to meat.

| Catalyst | What it is | Where it lives on our map | Setting |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Practical teaching | Biblical truth taught with clear application | The milk content: Starting Point, Foundations and the two milk modules; the meat modules' teaching | Church groups |
| Private disciplines | Personal prayer, Scripture and other spiritual practices | The daily rhythm (God Show Me, God Teach Me, God Change Me); Sabbath and fasting after review | On your own |
| Personal ministry | Serving others, especially beyond your comfort | The practice that turns milk into meat (Heb 5:14): each meat module's paired practice and LDP's Learning Labs | Mentors |

## **Entry assessment: a conversation, not a test**

The assessment is one sponsor-led conversation plus three to four weeks of observed practice. It places each topic on four marks taken from Hebrews 5 and 2 Timothy 2\. The marks match the briefing's Steps 1–4: Milk is milk, Growing is practice, Meat is meat, and Multiplying is sent. The catechumenate's "examination of life" was relational, and ours should be too.

| Mark | What it looks like | Text |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Milk | Can say what it is | Heb 5:12, τὰ στοιχεῖα τῆς ἀρχῆς (the elements of-the beginning) |
| Growing | Practices it regularly | Heb 5:14, διὰ τὴν ἕξιν (through the practice) |
| Meat | Discerns with it in real situations | Heb 5:14, πρὸς διάκρισιν καλοῦ τε καὶ κακοῦ (toward discernment of-good both and of-evil) |
| Multiplying | Can pass it on to someone else | 2 Tim 2:2 |

### **How it runs**

1. **Self-reflection first.** The person marks themselves on a one-page sheet. That lowers the stakes and sets the agenda.  
2. **One 60–90 minute conversation** with a sponsor or elder couple, working from one question per topic (below).  
3. **Observed practice over 3–4 weeks** in an ordinary group: praying aloud, walking through a passage, sharing a testimony.  
4. **Agree the placement together.** Pick two or three topics to work on and revisit the placement yearly.

Guardrails: no scores are recorded publicly; the person chooses whether to retake a milk week; and the framing comes from Heb 5:12 itself, which was written to people who'd been in church a long time. Starting with our teaching teams would model that no one is above the conversation.

### **One opening question per topic**

* **0 Gospel (Starting Point):** How would you explain the cross to someone who's never heard of it?  
* **1 Repentance:** What has God been turning you from this past year?  
* **2 Faith:** Tell me about a time trusting God cost you something, or carried you through a loss.  
* **3 Baptism:** What did your baptism mean then, and what does it mean to you now?  
* **4 Laying on of hands:** Where has the church blessed or sent you, and what gifts do others see in you?  
* **5 Resurrection:** What difference does a raised body make to how you live in this one?  
* **6 Judgment:** If you stood before God today, what would you rest on?  
* **7 Apostles' teaching:** Walk me through how you'd study a passage you didn't understand.  
* **8 Fellowship:** Who's carrying your burdens, whose are you carrying, and who're you discipling?  
* **9 Breaking of bread:** What happens for you at the Lord's Table?  
* **10 Prayers:** Who do you pray for regularly, and how do you pray when you're under attack?

## **Leadership: ministry directors, elders and pastors**

Leaders should come from the meat end of the map. Paul's qualifications use the same growth language as Hebrews, so the map gives selection a record of watched growth instead of years served or visibility.

| Text | Greek | What it means for the map |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1 Tim 3:6, "not a novice, lest being puffed up with pride he fall into the same condemnation as the devil" | νεόφυτον (newly-planted) | A milk-stage believer isn't an elder candidate yet |
| 1 Tim 3:2, "able to teach" | διδακτικόν (able-to-teach) | Hebrews says they "ought to be teachers" (5:12); elders are people who've gotten there |
| 1 Tim 3:10, "But let these also first be tested" | δοκιμαζέσθωσαν πρῶτον (let-them-be-tested first) | Observed practice over time is the test |
| 1 Tim 5:22, "Do not lay hands on anyone hastily" | χεῖρας ταχέως μηδενὶ ἐπιτίθει (hands quickly on-no-one lay) | Laying on of hands (\#4) is the last step, not a shortcut |

| Role | Biblical pattern | What the map should show |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Ministry directors | Acts 6:3; 1 Tim 3:8–13: good reputation, full of the Spirit and wisdom, tested first | Solid milk on every topic; meat and multiplying in the topics their ministry leans on |
| Elders | 1 Tim 3:1–7; Titus 1:5–9; 1 Pet 5:1–4 | Meat across the map; multiplying in fellowship (\#8) and teaching (\#7); commissioned (\#4) |
| Pastors | Elders who "labor in the word and doctrine" (1 Tim 5:17); Titus 1:9 | All of that, plus meat in the gospel (\#0) and in cutting straight (\#7) |

* **Cultivation:** Future elders are grown on purpose along the same path: Pathway B, LDP (the Leadership Development Program), sponsoring new believers, a ministry role, then elder.  
* **Guardrail:** The map is evidence, not a scorecard. Most of 1 Timothy 3 is character (temperate, gentle, not greedy, a well-run household, a good reputation outside); "able to teach" is the clearest skill. The map can't measure character, so people who know the candidate still weigh it. LDP's Leadership Agreement already sets Faircreek's character standards (private disciplines, believers baptism, Give/Serve/Love, a relational covenant, conduct), so selection can point to it rather than invent a new one.  
* **Our proposal, for discussion:** Putting ministry directors under the deacon pattern, and the role-by-role thresholds above. Faircreek's own role definitions apply here too.  
* **Women and leadership, where we've landed (14 Nov):** Steven Thorsen has settled that the lead pastor must be male, and every Faircreek elder now serves under a complementarian philosophy. Still open is whether women serve as non-voting advisors to the Board of Elders (BOE), to counterbalance an all-male board. Churches do this today: New Life PCA's female advisors attend session meetings in one-year terms with no vote, and Covenant College has a Women Advisors Board beside its all-male trustees. Critics, such as an Australian Presbyterian piece, warn it can blur the elder role into a two-tier system. Framing it as diaconal and advisory, with voice but no vote, answers that, and seats could go to women at the meat end who are already multiplying. Legally, the BOE is also Faircreek's corporate board, so the direct route is ex officio seats in Faircreek's regulations under Ohio Revised Code (ORC) §1702.27: such seats have no vote and don't count for quorum unless the documents say otherwise. A starting proposal is the Director of Women's Ministry plus up to two women the elders appoint for one-year terms, with voice but no vote and no seat in executive sessions. It takes an amendment through Faircreek's own process (by ORC §1702.11, a majority of voting members if the documents are silent), an Ohio attorney's review of the wording, and a check that directors-and-officers insurance covers them, since an ex officio seat likely carries a director's duties.
